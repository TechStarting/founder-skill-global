# Country pack: <country> (<ISO code>)

Dated: <YYYY-MM-DD>. Written by: <name or handle>. Reviewed by someone who runs a
business there: <yes, who | not yet>.

Rules for a pack:

- Every figure has an official source (the regulator or the government's own
  site) and the date it was checked. Figures that change often are marked
  `(changes often)`.
- Write what a founder must check, not what they must do. This is not legal, tax
  or financial advice.
- Describe buying behaviour that can be verified. No stereotypes about groups.
- Keep each section to what changes the work of that skill in this country.

## Basics

- Currency, symbol, and how prices are normally written.
- Languages buyers use, and which the business should publish in.
- How income is normally quoted (monthly or annual).
- Where to find official statistics on income and population.

## For the board and the idea

- What makes this market different from the frameworks' home market: size,
  purchasing power, how fragmented it is, how people find new businesses.
- The local incumbents a new business is usually compared with.

## For competitors

- Where local businesses are listed and reviewed (maps, marketplaces, delivery
  apps, social platforms).
- Languages to search in.

## For the consumer panel

- How to describe segments so they match this country: income per month or per
  year, and the official source for income bands.
- Buying behaviours that matter here (cash or QR, delivery apps, group buying,
  messaging app ordering).
- Common objections for this market, to be confirmed with real buyers.

## For pricing and the offer

- Whether prices are shown with or without tax.
- Price-display and discount rules, consumer guarantee rules.
- Common payment methods and what they cost the merchant.

## For the CFO

- Sales or consumption taxes: rates, who must register, the threshold.
- Invoicing rules (for example mandatory e-invoicing) and who is exempt.
- Payroll costs on top of wages: pension, social security, insurance, levies.
- The minimum wage.
- Company income tax, at the level of "ask the accountant about X".

## For marketing and brand

- The channels buyers actually use, in order.
- The trademark registry and how to search it.
- Domain endings, and advertising rules for claims.

## For operations

- How to register the business, and the main legal forms.
- Licences and permits by business type, and typical lead times.
- Data protection law.
- Sector rules (food, health, finance, alcohol).

## For launch

- The trading calendar: festivals, school holidays, paydays, seasons that move
  sales.
- The cheapest real-world tests that work here.

## Confirm with a professional

The items that need an accountant, a lawyer or the authority.
