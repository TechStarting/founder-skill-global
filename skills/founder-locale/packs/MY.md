# Country pack: Malaysia (MY)

Dated: 2026-11-12. Reviewed by someone who runs a business there: not yet.

Figures marked `(changes often)` have moved at least once in the last two years.
Re-check each against the official source in the Sources table before using it.
This pack is a checklist of what to confirm. It is not legal, tax or financial
advice.

## Basics

- **Currency**: Malaysian ringgit. Write prices as `RM6.50`. The ISO code is MYR.
- **Languages**: Bahasa Malaysia, English, Mandarin and Tamil are all in daily
  commercial use, and many buyers switch between them. Decide which the business
  publishes in from who the first customers are, and say so in the idea file.
- **Income is quoted per month**, not per year. Describe panel segments by
  monthly household or personal income.
- **Statistics**: the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) publishes
  household income by state and district. Use it for income bands instead of
  guessing.

## For the board and the idea

- The market is small next to the home markets of the three board frameworks.
  A niche that supports a business in the United States may be too thin here on
  its own, so the Monopoly lens should ask early whether the plan depends on
  expanding to neighbouring countries.
- Small and medium businesses are numerous and fragmented, and many decisions
  are made by an owner on a phone, not by a department.
- Trust travels through communities and messaging groups. Ask how the first
  customers will hear about the business from someone they already trust.

## For competitors

- **Where to look**: Google Maps listings and reviews, Facebook pages and
  groups, Instagram, TikTok, Xiaohongshu for Chinese-speaking buyers, Shopee and
  Lazada for goods, and GrabFood, foodpanda and ShopeeFood for food.
- **Search in more than one language.** A competitor invisible in English may be
  the market leader in Malay or Chinese.
- Delivery-app menus show a competitor's prices, but those are often marked up
  against the in-store price. Record which price you took.

## For the consumer panel

- Write segment incomes per month in ringgit.
- Behaviours worth including where they fit the business: pays by QR or e-wallet,
  orders through a delivery app, orders by messaging the shop on WhatsApp, buys
  on marketplace sale days, follows a seller's live stream.
- For food and drink, state plainly in the pitch whether the business is halal
  certified, Muslim-friendly or neither. Buyers will ask, and a vague answer
  reads as a no.
- The panel is still simulated. Malaysian buyers are best confirmed with a
  WhatsApp pre-order list, a pop-up stall or a paid pilot.

## For pricing and the offer

- **Service tax** is charged by registered businesses on taxable services. As of
  this pack: 8% standard, with food and beverage among the services at 6%
  `(changes often)`. A business below the registration threshold does not charge
  it. Say whether the planned price includes it.
- A service charge shown on a restaurant bill is the business's own charge, not
  a tax. Decide whether to have one and show it clearly.
- **Payment methods**: DuitNow QR, FPX online banking, cards, and e-wallets such
  as Touch 'n Go eWallet, GrabPay, Boost and ShopeePay. Each has a merchant fee:
  get the rate from the provider and put it in the CFO's variable costs.
- Guarantees, refunds and advertised discounts fall under consumer protection
  law. No "was RM X" price that was never charged.

## For the CFO

All of these are `(changes often)`. Confirm each before it goes into
`founder/numbers.json`.

- **Service tax registration**: the general threshold is RM500,000 of taxable
  services in 12 months. Food and beverage operators have a higher threshold,
  reported as RM1.5 million since the 2025 expansion. Sales tax on manufactured
  and imported goods is separate, at 5% or 10%.
- **E-invoicing (MyInvois)**: mandatory in phases by annual turnover. The
  exemption threshold was raised to RM3 million from 1 September 2026, after
  being RM500,000 and then RM1 million. Related-company conditions apply. A new
  small business is probably exempt today, but check, and check again each year.
- **Minimum wage**: RM1,700 a month. An increase to RM2,000 from June 2027 has
  been reported, with conditions for smaller businesses: confirm before
  planning year 2.
- **Payroll costs on top of wages**, for each employee:
  - EPF (KWSP): employer 13% on monthly wages up to RM5,000 and 12% above;
    employee 11%. Foreign workers: 2% employer and 2% employee since October
    2025.
  - SOCSO (PERKESO) and the Employment Insurance System: employer and employee
    contributions on wages up to a RM6,000 ceiling. Use the official
    contribution table.
  - HRD Corp levy for employers above a headcount threshold: check whether it
    applies.
- Put the employer's share of all of these in `fixed_monthly` next to wages. A
  plan that lists wages alone understates staff cost.
- Company and personal income tax, and whether to register as a sole
  proprietor, partnership or Sdn Bhd: ask an accountant.

## For marketing and brand

- **Channels**: WhatsApp (including WhatsApp Business) for orders and customer
  service, Facebook and its groups, Instagram, TikTok, Xiaohongshu, Google Maps
  for anything with a location, and delivery or marketplace apps as discovery
  channels in their own right.
- **Trademark**: search and register with the Intellectual Property Corporation
  of Malaysia (MyIPO). A registration elsewhere does not protect a name here.
- **Domains**: `.my` and `.com.my` alongside `.com`.
- **Claims**: health, halal and earnings claims are regulated. "Halal" may be
  used only with certification recognised by JAKIM. Do not imply it.

## For operations

- **Register the business** with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM):
  sole proprietorship or partnership, a limited liability partnership, or a
  private limited company (Sdn Bhd).
- **Premises**: a business premise licence and a signboard licence from the
  local council for the area. Requirements, fees and lead times differ between
  councils, so ask the one that covers the address before signing a lease.
- **Food businesses**: food handler training and typhoid vaccination for staff
  who handle food, and premises registration with the health authority. Halal
  certification is voluntary and takes time: decide early.
- **Alcohol, tobacco, health and financial services** each have their own
  licensing. Holding or moving customers' money is regulated by Bank Negara
  Malaysia.
- **Personal data**: the Personal Data Protection Act applies to customer data.
  Amendments in force since 2025 added breach notification, data protection
  officer rules and duties on processors. Sending data abroad has its own rules.
- **Tools**: choose payment, point of sale and accounting tools that support
  DuitNow QR and, if the business is above the threshold, e-invoicing.

## For launch

- **Calendar**: Ramadan and Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Chinese New Year, Deepavali,
  Christmas and year end, school holidays, and marketplace sale days all move
  demand. Ramadan changes eating hours and footfall for a whole month. The
  dates of several festivals shift every year: look them up for the launch year.
- **Paydays**: many salaries arrive at month end, and spending follows. Avoid
  launching a paid offer in the last week before payday.
- **Cheap real tests**: a stall at a weekend market or a pasar malam, a WhatsApp
  pre-order list with a price and a collection date, a listing on a delivery
  app from a shared kitchen, or three paid pilot customers for a service.

## Confirm with a professional

- Legal form, tax registration and whether service tax applies: an accountant.
- Whether e-invoicing applies this year: an accountant, or LHDN's guideline.
- Premises and food licences: the local council and the district health office.
- The trademark search result: a trademark agent.
- Anything involving customers' money, health claims or personal data at scale:
  a lawyer.
